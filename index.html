Embodiment and LLM agents: what we have found

Primary studies that compare embodied and non-embodied LLM agents on how people perceive them, and the motivation research behind our future-self app. A companion to the 3D/XR Embodied Agent Review Database, which collects the review-level literature.

What we have found so far

Direct comparisons

Five studies, all small and single-session

Five LLM studies compare an agent with a visible face or body against text or voice. Each ran one session with 20 to 92 people.

Direct comparisons

Engagement moves; judgments mostly do not

The embodied agent won on engagement or answer length in two studies and on preference in a third, but never on perceived competence, satisfaction or trust. In one, the text agent was rated more competent.

Direct comparisons

Few comparisons isolate the body

Only two keep the voice the same while adding a body or face. The other three also change voice, task or immersion, so the effect of the body itself is confounded.

Direct comparisons

Delay and uncanniness keep coming up

Three of the five report complaints about response latency, lip-sync or an uncanny avatar, which may cancel out what the body adds.

Mechanism studies

Nonverbal behaviour changes what words mean

Nonverbal cues that contradict speech change how the words are read, and embodied cues can raise trust in an agent’s hallucination warnings.

Motivation

For weeks-long goals, eager framing looks safer than fear

Fear messages work on average but least for repeated behaviours, and avoidance goals drain self-regulation over a month. Future-self app effects faded within months.

How records were found

Records come from targeted web, publisher and arXiv searches up to the update date, seeded from the review database and from citation chasing.

Findings are summarised from each paper’s abstract or full text as available. Records marked To screen have not been read yet.

Groups follow the review database: direct comparisons of an embodied and a non-embodied agent, mechanism studies within embodied agents, and a robot strand kept for future work.
